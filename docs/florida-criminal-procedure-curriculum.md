# Florida Criminal Procedure curriculum

Florida Criminal Procedure appears as a subject within Overview of Florida Law.
The drills use the course materials to select topics and official Florida rules
to verify the recall targets. They contain original short-answer prompts; the
source PDFs and multiple-choice question bank are not distributed with the app.

## Rule verification

The reference is the [Florida Rules of Criminal Procedure effective October 1,
2026](https://www-media.floridabar.org/uploads/2026/10/2027_04-Oct-Criminal-Procedure-Rules-10-1-2026.pdf).
Source discrepancies are explained beside the relevant drills. In particular:

- Rule 3.191 starts the ordinary 90-day misdemeanor and 175-day felony clocks
  with filing the formal charging document. A valid demand uses a 60-day clock.
  The notice-of-expiration process includes a hearing within five days and a
  30-day recapture period. Procedural dismissal is without prejudice; a
  constitutional speedy-trial violation requires dismissal with prejudice.
  The current calendar-call rule sets trial between five and sixty days after
  the demand; the former forty-five-day upper limit is not used.
- Rule 3.210 prohibits proceeding through material stages while a defendant is
  incompetent. The slide statement that an incompetent defendant can be tried
  is erroneous. Competency concerns the present ability to participate; insanity
  concerns the defendant's mental state when the offense occurred.
- Rule 3.350 grants ten ordinary peremptory challenges for an offense punishable
  by death or life imprisonment, six for other felonies, and three for
  misdemeanors. The ten-challenge category is broader than capital cases.
- Rule 3.133 requires extraordinary circumstances for an extension of the
  48-hour nonadversary probable-cause deadline. Release at this stage does not
  itself dismiss the prosecution.
- Rule 3.190(c) uses the motion-to-dismiss deadline set by the court, subject
  to the rule's exceptions. The source's at-or-before-arraignment deadline is
  not the current recall target.
