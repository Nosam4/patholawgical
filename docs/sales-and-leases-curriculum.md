# Sales and Leases question bank

The bank adds 64 drills with 397 scored blanks to RulePath. The three existing hybrid-transaction flowcharts remain in their existing subject with their identifiers unchanged, for 67 Sales & Leases questions overall. New content consists of 60 Sporcle grids and four flowcharts. It follows the eight sections of the finished outline, supplemented by the available course slides, assigned problems, and case materials.

## Substantive corrections and qualifications

The following distinctions were used in drafting instead of copying compressed or conflicting outline statements. Public authorities were checked September 14, 2026. Cornell's UCC pages reproduce model provisions; they are not a claim that every state has identical current law. Florida's hybrid statute is identified separately.

1. **Trade practice and scope.** Private trade customs do not themselves create a categorical diamond- or grain-industry exemption. Ragus illustrates interpreting “24/case” using trade usage, not a general ten-day statutory cure period. Agreement context comes before default delivery terms. See [§ 1-303](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/1/1-303).
2. **Formation and firm offers.** Open terms require both intent to contract and a reasonably certain basis for a remedy. Firm offers require a signed assurance; the three-month cap concerns irrevocability without consideration. The merchant category for these business practices is not the special merchantability category. See [§ 2-204](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-204), [§ 2-205](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-205), and the course's § 2-104 Comment 2 discussion.
3. **Forms and confirmations.** Section 2-207 includes written confirmations of an existing agreement. Subsection (3) governs contracts established by both parties' conduct where writings do not establish one. Treatment of different terms under subsection (2) is jurisdiction-dependent; the course's knockout approach is identified accordingly. See [§ 2-207](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-207).
4. **Warranty disclaimers and priority.** Express warranties and disclaimers are read consistently where reasonable, with negation ineffective to the extent unreasonable. A sales merchantability disclaimer under subsection (2) may be oral; a fitness disclaimer requires a conspicuous writing. Article 2A imposes different form requirements, including written/conspicuous as-is language. The sample priority in § 2-317(b) concerns a sample from an existing bulk. See [§ 2-316](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-316), [§ 2-317](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-317), and [§ 2A-214](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2A/2A-214).
5. **Magnuson-Moss.** A supplier's written warranty or qualifying service contract triggers § 2308; a duration restriction is not automatic. Full warranties prohibit the warrantor's implied-warranty duration limit under § 2304(a)(2). Prior resort to an informal process requires the statutory conditions, not simply any process the supplier proposes. Fees are subject to the court's statutory discretion. Conflicting outline summaries of McCurdy/McNamara are not scored as a universal rule about written warranties or privity. See [§ 2304](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/2304), [§ 2308](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/2308), and [§ 2310](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/2310).
6. **Privity.** Alternative C does not permit the seller to exclude this section's operation for an individual's personal injury; the outline reverses that point. Beneficiary status does not itself establish breach. Remote-seller privity and notice questions remain tied to governing state law. See [§ 2-318](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-318).
7. **Leases and creditors.** A true lease need not include a termination right. Failure of the bright-line test leads to the economic inquiry used in class. The test evaluates expectations at inception. Filing alone does not create an enforceable security interest or eliminate bankruptcy procedures. The statutory independent-promises rule is for nonconsumer finance leases after acceptance. See [§ 1-203](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/1/1-203), [§ 9-505](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/9/9-505), and [§ 2A-407](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2A/2A-407).
8. **Title.** Voidable title requires delivery under a transaction of purchase; voluntary possession alone is insufficient. Entrustment is a separate doctrine. Failure of its requirements does not automatically turn all title “void.” The ordinary-course definition is § 1-201(b)(9). See [§ 2-403](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-403).
9. **Acceptance, cure, and revocation.** Ratification is needed for an inconsistent ownership act if it is wrongful against the seller. Acceptance alone does not eliminate warranty remedies. Known-defect revocation requires the qualifying expectation of cure. Cure after revocation is not presented as an automatic § 2-508 right. Case-based shaken-faith and rental-value discussions are not transformed into universal requirements. See [§ 2-606](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-606), [§ 2-607](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-607), and [§ 2-608](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-608).
10. **Remedies and risk.** Rejection/revocation does not categorically preclude expectation damages. Nonnegotiable bailee documents require the § 2-503(4)(b) qualifications; receipt alone is incomplete. The two breach-risk provisions refer to different parties' insurance and the buyer-breach rule has a commercially reasonable time limit. See [§ 2-711](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-711), [§ 2-503](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-503), and [§ 2-510](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-510).
11. **Model law and Florida.** The four-year § 2-725 drill is expressly labeled as the course's model UCC rule, including the future-performance exception. The new Florida hybrid drill follows the enacted [§ 672.102](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/672.102) and assumes that amended statute governs the transaction. Older hybrid tests remain available as comparative doctrines.
