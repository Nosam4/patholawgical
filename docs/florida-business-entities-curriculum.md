# Florida Business Entities curriculum

Business Entities is a subject in Overview of Florida Law. The 42 original
short-answer drills contain 299 recall blanks across corporations, general and
limited partnerships, LLPs/LLLPs, and LLCs. Course materials determine scope;
official statutes and decisions verify the legal targets. The application does
not redistribute the source PDFs or their proprietary hypotheticals.

## Course sources and scope

- Business Entities Daily Schedule.png: the October 14–23 unit includes
  corporations, partnerships, and LLCs. The assigned reading numbers are printed
  page numbers, not necessarily PDF page numbers.
- 01 Blank Corporations FL Presentation.pdf: 32 PDF pages; formation, powers,
  financing, shareholders, directors, fundamental changes, and dissolution.
- 01 Blank Corporations FL Outline.pdf: 7 PDF pages; the corresponding slide
  outline, dated April 6, 2025.
- FL.Geis.Corporations.pdf: 48 PDF pages. Corporate doctrines appear in PDF
  pages 1–39; special forms in pages 39–42; LLCs in pages 42–47. The federal
  securities supplement follows chapter 3, PDF pages 12–15.
- FL.Geis.Partnerships.pdf: 22 PDF pages, with substantive selected coverage
  through PDF page 21, including general partnerships and special forms.

Each drill's **Course material** panel lists its relevant PDF pages. Page numbers
always refer to the PDF page counter. The visible source link opens the principal
statutory provision or federal rule, and the panel identifies related authorities.
Some short statutory supplements are expressly identified. No additional
partnership slides or unit practice-question answer key were available in the
BusinessEntities folder.

The material covers the principal doctrines in the supplied unit. It is selected
recall practice, rather than a full transcription of the handouts or a prediction
of exam frequency. It does not attempt exhaustive securities-registration,
tender-offer, tax-eligibility, nonprofit, or professional-entity coverage.

## Verified legal distinctions

Targets were checked against the 2026 Florida Statutes on October 16, 2026.
Statutory defaults are qualified where a valid agreement or governing document
can change them. Significant source clarifications include:

- Annual-report failure bars prosecuting or maintaining an action until filing
  and payment of fees and penalties. The slide's added defense bar is not the
  current chapter 607 rule. [§ 607.1622](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/607.1622)
- Corporate derivative pleading allows particularized reasons no demand was made;
  the slides' demand-only summary is incomplete. Current standing permits
  qualifying successor status through transfer **or** operation of law.
  [§ 607.0742](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/607.0742),
  [§ 607.0741](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/607.0741)
- Authorized share classes collectively must provide unlimited voting and
  net-asset rights. The slide's “limited rights” phrase is misleading.
  [§ 607.0601](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/607.0601)
- A business corporation ordinarily needs at least one director. Board quorum
  counts the fixed board, including vacancies; the permitted floor is one third.
  [§ 607.0803](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/607.0803),
  [§ 607.0824](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/607.0824)
- Fundamental-change votes depend on the particular action. An ordinary article
  amendment generally uses a majority-entitled quorum with favorable votes
  exceeding opposing votes; merger approval generally requires a majority of
  votes entitled. The slide's one-size majority-of-outstanding rule is overbroad.
  [§ 607.1003](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/607.1003),
  [§ 607.1103](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/607.1103)
- Qualified conflict approval can shift the fairness burden rather than provide
  automatic immunity. Successful-defense indemnification and permissive
  indemnification differ; articles can prospectively limit rights. Judicial
  dissolution requires statutory conditions beyond a bare assertion of deadlock.
  [§ 607.0832](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/607.0832),
  [§ 607.0858](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/607.0858),
  [§ 607.1430](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/607.1430)
- Veil piercing requires domination, fraudulent or improper use, and resulting
  injury. Thin capitalization alone is not an automatic rule. The Geis tip
  against LLC veil piercing is too categorical: Florida decisions apply the
  doctrine to LLCs. The app teaches the liability shield with that qualification.
  [Segal v. Forastero, Inc. (Fla. 3d DCA 2021)](https://flcourts-media.flcourts.gov/content/download/745970/opinion/210089_DC13_06022021_102345_i.pdf)
- General-partnership losses follow specified profit shares by default. Florida's
  post-dissociation liability tail is **one year**, subject to outsider conditions,
  rather than the two-year period sometimes encountered in model-act summaries.
  [§ 620.8401](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/620.8401),
  [§ 620.8703](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/620.8703)
- LP certificates specify LLLP status; duration is not a required item in the
  current statute. Limited-partner status alone imposes no fiduciary duty, but
  delegated management powers create role-specific duties.
  [§ 620.1201](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/620.1201),
  [§ 620.1305](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/620.1305)
- Florida LLC member voting ordinarily tracks profit interests; manager voting
  uses manager headcount. Default manager action without a meeting requires
  unanimous consent in a record. LLC duty modifications have nonwaivable limits;
  express-will withdrawal before winding up is wrongful by default, and economic
  transfer alone does not confer membership or management rights.
  [§ 605.04073](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/605.04073),
  [§ 605.0105](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/605.0105),
  [§ 605.0601](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/605.0601)

The federal supplement distinguishes private Rule 10b-5 fraud elements from
the short-swing-profit remedy under § 16(b), without relying on the handout's
simplified issuer-registration thresholds.
[Rule 10b-5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/240.10b-5),
[Halliburton, 573 U.S. 258 (2014)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-573/pdf/USREPORTS-573-258.pdf),
[15 U.S.C. § 78p](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/78p)

## Implementation and checks

`src/floridaBusinessEntitiesQuestions.js` contains the corporate drills and
assembles the subject; `src/floridaBusinessEntitiesUnincorporated.js` contains
the partnerships and LLCs. The modules explicitly classify each drill's minimum
review level. Sporcle grids use active-blank matching so a generic answer cannot
fill unrelated legal concepts elsewhere in the drill.

Curriculum tests check catalog integration, all review depths, IDs, sources,
canonical and alias scoring, and meaningful entity distinctions. Browser checks
cover depth selection, saved progress, active-blank behavior, source panels, and
revealed answer layouts on desktop and mobile.
